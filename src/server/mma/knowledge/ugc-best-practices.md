## 7. Video realism — the single most important section

Every product prompt lives or dies on whether it reads as a real-person video or an ad (paid content). The client has been explicit: ad-feel tanks reach. Here is what separates the two.

**Ad-feel signals (never include these):**
- A "hook" line delivered directly to camera at 0s
- Golden-hour cinematic lighting
- A cobbled Mediterranean street, boutique dressing room, luxe hotel bathroom setting
- Wink to camera at the end
- Hands-up "big excited grin" pose at the finale
- "Yellow basket" spoken as a punchy CTA
- "Go and get it" delivered with energy
- Bottle presented deliberately to camera with the label centred
- Consistent eye contact with the lens throughout
- Perfectly styled hair and makeup
- Glossy, professional-sounding delivery
- Every sentence on-message
- Clear four-beat structure: hook → reveal → benefit → CTA

**Real-video-feel signals (build every prompt around these):**
- Opens mid-thought — like the viewer caught the video already running
- Inconsistent eye contact — she looks at the bottle, off-camera, at her hands, only meeting the lens 30–40% of the time
- Brand name buried inside a story, not announced
- One personal anecdote (mum stole it, boyfriend keeps sniffing me, someone at work asked)
- No CTA at the end — the closing line trails off with a shrug or a small "yeah, I'm obsessed" that lands flat, not punchy
- Bottle held casually — thigh level or chest height — not raised to camera
- Bare face or minimal makeup
- Loose hair, sweatshirt, real fabric — not styled
- Real setting: bedroom, kitchen, parked car, not a luxury bathroom
- Natural micro-pauses, half-laughs under the breath, the occasional "um"
- Normal indoor daylight, not golden hour
- The bottle never rotates or gets shown label-forward like a product shot
- Static phone-on-furniture framing, not selfie held at arm's length

**The one-line test:** If the character ends the clip with more energy than she started it with, it reads as an ad. Real people finish anecdotes with a shrug, not a flourish.

**The "third bottle" close.** The strongest video endings imply value through a throwaway line — "this is my third bottle now," "happens every week," "I keep forgetting to film this." Never state the value. Let the viewer infer it.

## 8. Setting library — proven variations

Rotate through these so a content series doesn't feel like the same video twice.

**Bedroom (raw video baseline).** Cream linen bedding, plain wall, plant in corner, wooden dresser edge, morning daylight from one window. Character sits on the edge of the bed in an oversized sweatshirt.

**Kitchen (family/mum anecdotes).** Sage-grey shaker cabinets, quartz worktop, a plain ceramic mug of tea. Character leans against the counter. Late afternoon warm light.

**Parked car (post-outing anecdotes).** Cream leather interior, panoramic moonroof or sunroof for even light, plain dashboard. Engine off, gear in park. Bottle held at thigh level. Character in a bomber jacket or knit jumper.

**Luxury bathroom (aspirational/GRWM).** Marble, brass sconces, wall mirror. Only use when the client specifically wants the polished register. Higher ad-feel risk.

**Modern bedroom vanity (soft GRWM).** Wooden vanity desk, brass arm-lamp, small peony in a bud vase, plant in the corner. Fits well for the "morning routine" register.

**Walk-in closet / dressing room (evening prep).** Brass-framed mirror, cream velvet bench, hanging clothes rail softly out of focus. For "getting ready to go out" scripts.

**Post-workout bedroom (athleisure GRWM).** Cream bed, gym bag on the floor, character in a hoodie removing it to reveal a sports bra + gym leggings.

**Hotel bathroom (evening luxury).** Grey marble, black-framed mirror, brass sconces, a champagne flute on the counter. Fits the "sexy going-out" register.

**Glam corner (maximalist creator).** White shelving with pink ceramics, coffee-table books with pastel spines, small designer bags. Bright ring-light feel.

Every setting must respect the brand-bleed rule — no mugs with logos, no cereal boxes, no branded skincare bottles visible, no shopping bags with legible fonts.

## 10. Dialogue patterns — what to write and what to avoid

**Openings that work (mid-thought, no hook):**
- "Okay so I keep forgetting to film this and it's actually driving me mad."
- "Right — I genuinely have to hide this perfume from my mum at this point."
- "I genuinely just got stopped in Tesco."
- "Sorry — you walked past me and I had to refresh it before you asked." *(this one is the higher-energy variant — use sparingly)*
- "So I've been on a secret which I cannot gatekeep from you any longer." *(if going more scripted)*

**Openings that don't work (too ad-like):**
- "You know when you walk past a girl..." *(only works with the right register underneath — often reads scripted)*
- "If you don't like [X], do not buy this" *(strong opener but only if the delivery stays casual — very easy to sound performed)*

**Body copy patterns:**

*The scent trio.* "Coconut, peach, warm vanilla underneath" — the default. Delivered dreamy, eyes drift up.

*The "it's giving" trio.* "It's giving expensive, it's giving holiday, it's giving Love Island villa." Deliver each with a small punctuating flick of the hand.

*Compliment story.* "Was queuing up to pay and this woman behind me just leaned in and asked what I was wearing." Delivered like a small annoyance, not a flex.

*Family anecdote.* "My mum came in mine yesterday, found this on the side, sprayed it like six times — half the bottle's just gone." Mock-exasperated.

*Longevity anecdote.* "I sprayed it on my hoodie three days ago, washed it, came out the machine still smelling like it." Delivered as genuine disbelief.

**Closes that work (soft trail-off):**
- "Yeah. I'm obsessed."
- "So… yeah, this is my third bottle now."
- "Anyway. Happens like every week now."
- "Honestly, I'm not even surprised anymore."

**Closes that don't work (too punchy):**
- "Go and get it."
- "Yellow basket, half price, be quick."
- Any imperative-mood CTA delivered with lift in the voice.

**Timing math:** Natural British female TikTok pace is 3.5–4 words per second. At 15s, that's roughly 55–65 words. At 30s, roughly 105–120 words. Don't overpack.

When starting a new series, generate the character once with Nano Banana Pro at 2k or 4k in 9:16, then pass the resulting `image_id` as `start_image` to every Seedance clip in the series. This locks facial continuity.

## 11. Format library — proven concepts

Rotate through these to build a content series. Each is grounded in the client's top-performing TikTok data.

**Format 1: "Caught in a private moment" (raw video baseline).** She's just doing something normal — sat on the edge of her bed, at her kitchen counter, in her car — and starts telling the camera about the perfume mid-thought. One anecdote, no CTA, no polish. Setting: bedroom / kitchen / parked car. Length: 15s. Primary reach play.

**Format 2: "Cold approach anecdote."** She recounts being stopped in the street / at Tesco / at the coffee shop, and someone asking what she's wearing. Told as an after-the-fact story, not staged POV. Length: 15s.

**Format 3: "Family theft anecdote."** Her mum / sister / best friend keeps stealing the bottle. Kitchen setting. Length: 15s. Very high conversion — this is the format that hit £3.5k revenue in the client's proven data.

**Format 4: "Longevity anecdote."** The washed hoodie story, or the "came off holiday and it still smells" beat. Bedroom or kitchen. Length: 15s.

**Format 5: "GRWM finishing touches."** She does a small getting-ready beat (lip liner, moisturiser, earrings, hair brush, mascara) then reveals the perfume as the final step, sprays, and reacts. 14s beat-by-beat, static camera. Ends on a giggle-hands-up pose — this is the ONE format where the polished finale is fine because the visual grammar is different (fixed camera GRWM, not talking-head).

**Format 6: "Two-bottle showcase."** She sprays one, then holds up both the rose-gold and champagne-gold variants. Requires @image_1 (rose-gold) + @image_5 (champagne-gold) references. Length: 14s.

**Format 7: "Silent + music."** Fully silent Seedance generation, character in a maximalist glam setting doing a slow bottle showcase and spray. Client layers a trending audio in CapCut. Length: 5–10s. Fast reach play, low friction.

**Format 8: "Boyfriend hand-off."** Two-character: off-camera male voice hands her the Nyla bottle at 0–2s ("Are you happy?"). She reacts and reviews. Requires careful dialogue timing — her mouth stays still during his lines, moves only during hers. Length: 15s per clip, 30s across two clips.

**Format 9: "Multi-cut jumpcut showcase."** Multiple angles cut hard together — face close-up, wrist spray, mirror side-profile, final laugh. Cinematic register. Length: 15s. Use sparingly — higher production feel means higher ad-feel risk.

**Format 10: "Get-ready + reveal."** She takes a hoodie off after the gym, or steps out of the shower in a robe, or removes an oversized layer — revealing the sports bra / dress / evening look underneath. Then perfume spray as final step. Wardrobe transition is the visual hook.

## 12. The critical difference between formats 1 and 5

Format 1 (raw video baseline) and Format 5 (GRWM) use opposite grammar. Don't mix them.

Format 1 lives in inconsistency: mid-thought openings, drifting eye contact, no CTA, plain settings, casual bottle handling, "yeah, I'm obsessed" close.

Format 5 lives in structure: fixed static camera, beat-by-beat action, silent character (no dialogue), one giggle-and-hands-up finale pose, controlled framing, product-shot bottle reveal at 4-7s, spray with visible mist, dual-bottle showcase at 10-12s.

Format 5's polished finale is fine because it reads as GRWM-genre convention, not as an ad-CTA. But you can't put a giggle-and-hands-up finale on a Format 1 clip — that's what makes it feel like an ad.

## 14. Data-driven TikTok insights (from the client's proven data)

**Duration windows that perform:**
- <10s: pure reach plays, lower revenue but massive views
- 10–20s: reach + light conversion, healthy middle ground
- 25–50s: highest revenue per view, conversion sweet spot
- >60s: story-format clips, only for narrative anecdotes


**Hooks that reach hardest (highest views per impression):**
1. Cold approach ("what perfume are you wearing?") 
2. Ungatekeeping ("we don't gatekeep in this house") 
3. Deal shout ("TO £35 with coupons!") 
4. Pattern interrupt / negative hook ("do not buy this if you don't like attention")

**Hooks that convert hardest (highest revenue per view):**
1. Mum-daughter anecdote
2. Longevity anecdote (washed hoodie, boyfriend keeps asking)
3. GRWM finishing touches with Nyla as final step
4. Counterfeit warning

**Recurring conversion mechanics:**
- "Yellow basket" reference in the CTA
- Named price (£25 quid, half price)
- Scarcity ("flash sale," "won't last long")
- Named social proof (Huda from Love Island, 4.5 stars from X reviews)

- "Quid" over "pounds"
- "Gorgeous" over "beautiful"
- "Genuinely" as a natural filler
- "Insane" / "unreal" as descriptors
- Slight upspeak on emotional beats

## 16. Common mistakes — what I got wrong before this document existed

**Mistake 1: The bag said NYLA.** Original prompt described a "canvas tote bag" containing the perfume. Seedance bled the brand onto the bag. Fixed by removing all secondary props that could grow logos, and adding the explicit brand-bleed rule block at the top of every prompt.

**Mistake 2: The video felt like an ad.** Original prompt used golden-hour Mediterranean cobbled street, a "hook" line delivered to camera at 0s, a wink at the end, "Honestly, you're welcome" as a closing beat, cinematic warm grade. Every one of those signals is ad-coded. Fixed by switching to raw indoor video register — plain wall, mid-thought opening, drifting eye contact, closing on a flat "yeah, I'm obsessed."

**Mistake 3: The lip-sync was weird.** Bottle held near her face during dialogue beats. Fixed by keeping the bottle at chest or thigh level during speech and only raising it during silent beats.

**Mistake 4: Both sides of the bottle had NYLA on them.** Prompt described the bottle rotating, and separately described "Back face is smooth, undecorated rose-gold." Seedance renders whatever you describe — including the front decoration on both sides, when confused. Fixed by removing the back-face description entirely and never describing bottle rotation on camera.

**Mistake 5: Charity ad prompt was moderation-flagged.** Detailed descriptions of children under 10, developing-world poverty imagery, distress language. Fixed by aging up cast, removing specific child ages, softening emotional-distress language.
