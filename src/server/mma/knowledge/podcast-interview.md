# Podcast Interview Mode — Bulletproof Prompting Library

Use this mode for studio podcasts, video podcasts, founder interviews, conversational YouTube shows, remote-style discussions staged in one room, and short social clips extracted from a longer conversation. A believable podcast is not two people sitting beside decorative microphones. It is a stable spatial system: seats, eyelines, microphones, camera coverage, room acoustics, conversational turn-taking and edit logic all remain coherent.

Follow `seedance-prompting.md` first for blueprint order, media tags, dialogue timing, audio binding, negatives and continuity.

## 1. Select the format

- **Two-person conversational podcast:** equal host/guest presence, one wide and two singles.
- **Host-led interview:** host drives timing; guest gets longer close coverage.
- **Founder story:** restrained, thoughtful delivery, visual emphasis on hands and pauses.
- **Comedy podcast:** faster overlaps, wider reactions, room laughter only if real participants exist.
- **Panel:** three or more speakers; use wider stable coverage and stricter microphone/eyeline mapping.
- **Remote-call aesthetic:** screens/headphones/compression are visible and audible; do not pretend both people share one acoustic room.
- **Short social excerpt:** 15–60 seconds selected from a longer conversation; must feel like it starts mid-thought, not as a freshly performed hook.
- **Audio-first studio:** minimal camera movement, premium sound, practical set.

## 2. Design the room before the shots

Write a simple floor plan:

- host chair position;
- guest chair position;
- table size and shape;
- microphone boom-arm anchor points;
- camera A wide position;
- camera B host position;
- camera C guest position;
- key/fill/practical light positions;
- background depth and objects;
- acoustic treatment or soft surfaces.

Example:

> Host camera-left and guest camera-right, seated at a 105 cm round table, chairs angled 25 degrees toward each other. Camera A is centred beyond the table; Camera B sits just outside guest’s shoulder aimed at host; Camera C mirrors it aimed at guest. Both singles remain on the same side of the conversational axis.

## 3. Preserve the 180-degree line and eyelines

- Establish the axis through host and guest.
- Keep all normal coverage on one side of that axis.
- Host’s single should look slightly camera-right toward guest.
- Guest’s single should look slightly camera-left toward host.
- A wide shot proves the geography.
- Do not mirror faces or swap screen direction between cuts.
- If crossing the line is intentional, show a neutral transition or camera move that explains it.

Podcast close-ups usually look just off lens toward the conversation partner—not directly into camera.

## 4. Camera package and shot roles

### Camera A — wide/two-shot

- captures both people, table and microphones;
- used for setup, overlap, laughter, physical demonstrations and resets;
- often 24–35mm equivalent depending on room;
- eye level or slightly above table height;
- should not distort faces at the frame edge.

### Camera B — host single

- medium close-up or close-up;
- typically 50–85mm equivalent;
- shows host’s eyes, mouth, shoulders and microphone edge;
- enough negative space toward guest.

### Camera C — guest single

- mirrors host coverage without being mechanically identical;
- can be marginally tighter for the primary storyteller;
- preserves the same light direction and room geography.

### Optional insert camera

- hands, notebook, object or control surface;
- never replaces essential speaker coverage during important dialogue;
- use during listening, pauses or voice-over.

## 5. Switching and edit logic

Do not switch cameras randomly every sentence.

Cut to:

- the person beginning a meaningful thought;
- listener reaction after the thought lands;
- wide shot for overlap, laughter or shared physical action;
- close-up for emotional admission or precise detail;
- insert only when the object changes meaning.

Useful rules:

- hold a speaker through a complete emotional beat;
- do not cut away exactly before the facial reaction;
- avoid constant ping-pong editing;
- use the wide shot as spatial punctuation;
- let audio continue seamlessly across cuts;
- in a 15-second generated clip, 2–4 camera changes are usually enough.

A social excerpt can begin mid-sentence, but the first image should already show who owns the thought.

## 6. Microphone geometry

Choose the visible microphone type and keep it stable.

### Dynamic broadcast microphone

- 5–15 cm from mouth depending on voice and gain;
- aimed slightly off-axis to reduce plosives;
- boom arm approaches from table side without covering the face;
- close placement creates fuller tone through proximity effect;
- head movement changes level and tone slightly.

### Condenser studio microphone

- more room-sensitive;
- requires credible acoustic treatment and lower background noise;
- usually placed with pop filter and controlled distance.

### Lavalier podcast

- cleaner frame but different visual grammar;
- clothing placement and rustle matter;
- do not show unused decorative broadcast microphones if lavaliers carry the sound.

Hard rules:

- one microphone per speaker unless the format intentionally shares;
- microphone does not move between cuts;
- boom arm joints and cables remain connected;
- mouth-to-mic distance changes audibly;
- no hand passes through microphone or table;
- speakers do not rotate away while retaining identical close-mic sound.

## 7. Audio capture and mix

A convincing podcast has separate speaker tracks even when the final mix is stereo.

Specify:

- host mic and guest mic recorded separately;
- each voice’s timbre and distance;
- room tone and noise floor;
- compression/limiting level;
- plosive and sibilance handling;
- cross-talk/bleed amount;
- headphones or no headphones;
- table/boom handling noise;
- whether the final output is raw or processed.

### Desired sound

- crisp consonants without brittle highs;
- full but not artificially sub-bass-heavy voice;
- stable level with natural dynamics;
- low room reflection;
- breath and mouth detail present but not exaggerated;
- listener’s small reactions audible below speaker;
- no music unless the show uses a deliberate intro/outro.

### Acoustic room clues

- rugs, curtains, upholstered chairs and acoustic panels reduce reflections;
- glass walls, bare tables and hard ceilings create stronger reflections;
- the visual room must support the audio. A giant glass loft cannot sound fully anechoic without visible treatment or close dynamic microphones.

## 8. Conversational performance

Give each speaker a different listening style.

Host may:

- maintain eye contact;
- nod once at a key point;
- glance at notes;
- lean toward mic for follow-up;
- avoid mouthing guest’s words.

Guest may:

- search for wording;
- look away briefly while recalling;
- gesture with one hand;
- lean back after a difficult admission;
- finish with an exhale rather than a polished smile.

Include natural:

- micro-pauses;
- breath before answer;
- one overlap or acknowledgment;
- incomplete sentence or self-correction when appropriate;
- unequal reaction timing.

Do not use constant nodding, frozen smiles or synchronized laughter.

## 9. Hands, table and prop continuity

Track:

- mug/glass positions and liquid levels;
- notebook orientation and pen hand;
- headphones, cable paths and boom arms;
- phone placement;
- sleeves around wrists;
- chair posture;
- whether hands are above or below the table.

Avoid too many props. Every reflective or text-bearing object increases failure risk.

## 10. Lighting

Common podcast setup:

- soft key 30–45 degrees to each face;
- controlled fill from opposite side;
- subtle back/edge separation;
- practical lamps in background;
- exposure matched across cameras;
- colour temperature consistent unless motivated.

Keep skin highlights natural and microphones readable. Do not place a strong practical directly behind a head unless intentional. Avoid a different grade on every camera.

## 11. Focus and depth

- wide shot: both speakers within usable depth;
- singles: focus fixed on near eye;
- microphone foreground can be slightly soft but not an amorphous blob;
- no random focus pulls during uninterrupted dialogue;
- if a person leans significantly, focus makes one small correction;
- shallow depth must not erase set geography completely.

## 12. AI tells and fixes

### Decorative microphones with impossible audio
Cause: microphones too far away or pointed elsewhere.
Fix: place each 5–15 cm from mouth and tie tone/level to movement.

### Camera-angle teleportation
Cause: cuts described without room geometry.
Fix: define cameras A/B/C and preserve the axis.

### Listener talks silently
Cause: both mouths animate during one line.
Fix: exact active-speaker and closed-mouth listener instructions.

### Synchronized nodding
Cause: generic “they react naturally.”
Fix: assign distinct, delayed reactions.

### Fake premium studio
Cause: neon sign, brick wall, Edison bulbs and perfect bokeh used as shorthand.
Fix: specify functional layout, acoustic surfaces and ordinary imperfections.

### Microphone/boom mutation
Cause: no anchor or geometry.
Fix: state arm attachment, joint positions and cable path.

### Overprocessed radio voice
Cause: “deep crisp podcast sound” without restraint.
Fix: ask for close dynamic mic, moderate compression, natural chest tone, controlled plosives, no artificial bass boost.

### Random multicam cuts
Cause: every sentence triggers a cut.
Fix: cut by conversational function and hold reactions.

### Impossible wide-shot clarity
Cause: same isolated close-mic voice while speaker turns far away.
Fix: maintain microphone relation or let off-axis tone change.

## 14. Research principles incorporated

Professional video-podcast guidance recommends a reliable wide shot plus separate close coverage, cameras near eye level, even lighting and separately controlled microphone tracks. Recording guidance also emphasizes close microphone placement and acoustic treatment. This module turns those production principles into explicit spatial and audio continuity rules.

Preserve natural breaths, one chair creak, slight mouth moisture, skin pores, small asymmetrical brow movement, minor knit and overshirt folds, one delayed host nod, a tiny shift in guest mic proximity and ordinary room noise floor. Keep all audio clean enough for a professional podcast.
